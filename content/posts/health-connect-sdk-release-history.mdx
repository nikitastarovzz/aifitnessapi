Google's Health Connect Jetpack library, `androidx.health.connect:connect-client`,
has 32 releases on Google's [Jetpack release notes
page](https://developer.android.com/jetpack/androidx/releases/health-connect).
31 of them are pre-releases. The one stable build, 1.1.0, arrived on October 8,
2025, more than three years after the first release under that coordinate.

Every number in this post is computed from this site's [connect-client release
dataset](/health-connect-releases), which our generator read from Google's page
on October 3, 2026. Google's footer on the page said it was last updated on
August 26, 2026. Where this post quotes a release, it quotes Google's notes as
the dataset stores them.

## The whole history in one table

| Series | Alpha | Beta | RC | Stable | First release | Last release |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | 8 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1.0.0-alpha04, Aug 24, 2022 | 1.0.0-alpha11, Feb 22, 2023 |
| 1.1 | 12 | 2 | 3 | 1 | 1.1.0-alpha01, Jun 7, 2023 | 1.1.0, Oct 8, 2025 |
| 1.2 | 6 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1.2.0-alpha01, Jul 30, 2025 | 1.2.0-alpha06, Aug 26, 2026 |
| **Total** | **26** | **2** | **3** | **1** | | |

By calendar year, the releases fall 5 in 2022, 9 in 2023, 4 in 2024, 10 in 2025
and 4 so far in 2026.

## It starts at alpha04 because the artifact was renamed

The history begins at 1.0.0-alpha04, not alpha01. Google's notes for that
release, dated August 24, 2022, open with a section label that reads, exactly
as Google prints it, stray backtick included: "Migration to
\`androidx.health.connect". Re-read on October 4, 2026, the paragraph under it
says: "As of 1.0.0-alpha04, androidx.health:health-connect-client was migrated
to androidx.health.connect:connect-client."

The next paragraph tells you to change the dependency from
`androidx.health:health-connect-client:1.0.0-alpha03` to
`androidx.health.connect:connect-client:1.0.0-alpha04`. Older versions are on
the old coordinate's release page. The dataset counts only releases whose own
release paragraph names `androidx.health.connect:connect-client`.

## 1.0 never shipped a stable build

The 1.0 series ran eight alphas in 182 days, from 1.0.0-alpha04 to 1.0.0-alpha11
on February 22, 2023. Then nothing for 105 days, and the next release was
1.1.0-alpha01 on June 7, 2023. There is no 1.0.0 in Google's notes.

1.1.0-alpha01's first note explains the jump: "Support for the Android 14
framework version of Health Connect. This SDK is a prerequisite for Android 14.
Apps will not be able to integrate with Health Connect on Android 14 without
it." Its release paragraph adds: "This version is developed in an internal
branch."

## 1.1 spent 630 days in alpha

1.1 went through twelve alphas, from 1.1.0-alpha01 on June 7, 2023 to
1.1.0-alpha12 on February 26, 2025: 630 days. The series also contains the
longest silence in the dataset, 238 days between 1.1.0-alpha07 (January 10,
2024) and 1.1.0-alpha08 (September 4, 2024). 2024 had only four releases, all
1.1 alphas.

Some of what shipped in that run is still central to how apps read data. The
notes for 1.1.0-alpha09, dated September 18, 2024, open with: "Add background
reads permission, guarded by feature availability." 1.1.0-alpha11 (January 15,
2025) opens with: "Updated background and history read permissions to support
Android 13 and below." Those two permissions, and the rest of the platform's
strings, are covered in [Health Connect has 219
permissions](/blog/health-connect-219-permissions).

## Then stabilisation, which still added features

After alpha12 the pace changed. 1.1.0-beta01 followed 14 days later, on March
12, 2025. Then:

- **1.1.0-beta02** (April 9, 2025) has a New Features section. Its first note:
  "Added experimental Personal Health Record (PHR) APIs for reading and writing
  medical data, based on the Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources (FHIR®)
  format."
- **1.1.0-rc01** (April 23, 2025) has an API Changes section: "Added mindfulness
  feature availability flag for developers."
- **1.1.0-rc02** (June 4, 2025) and **1.1.0-rc03** (July 16, 2025) are bug-fix
  releases. rc03's note: "Fixed IllegalArgumentException for aggregations over
  a DST boundary."
- **1.1.0** (October 8, 2025), 84 days after rc03. Google's paragraph says the
  artifacts "have been promoted to its first stable release with no changes
  since its previous RC release."

That is 17 pre-releases and 854 days from 1.1.0-alpha01 to 1.1.0, or 1,141 days
from 1.0.0-alpha04. As of the October 3, 2026 read, 1.1.0 is still the only
stable release, 360 days after it shipped.

## 1.2 overlapped 1.1, and is still alpha

Google opened 1.2 before closing 1.1. 1.2.0-alpha01 is dated July 30, 2025, 70
days before 1.1.0 went stable, and 1.2.0-alpha02 came out on October 8, 2025,
the same day as 1.1.0. Google's page lists releases under their series heading,
so reading it top to bottom does not give you the date order.

Since then 1.2 has had four more alphas, with gaps of 168 days (to 1.2.0-alpha03,
March 25, 2026) and 112 days (to 1.2.0-alpha05, August 12, 2026). Some first
notes from the series:

- 1.2.0-alpha03: "Introduce new fields to ExerciseSessionRecord and
  ExerciseSegment to support richer exercise tracking"
- 1.2.0-alpha04: "Matchmaking APIs: Introduced new methods to help users
  discover and connect compatible data sources that can provide the health data
  your app needs."
- 1.2.0-alpha05: "The minimum SDK requirement for this library is now API 24
  (minSdk 24)."
- 1.2.0-alpha06: "Updated Health Connect release and dev signing certificates."

Six alphas over 392 days, no beta yet.

## Cadence: monthly on average, with long gaps

With releases sorted by date there are 31 distinct release dates (1.1.0 and
1.2.0-alpha02 share one). Across the 30 gaps between them:

- the median gap is 28 days;
- 9 gaps are exactly 14 days, and 16 of the 30 are 31 days or less;
- 5 gaps are longer than 90 days: 238, 168, 112, 105 and 91 days.

The release notes don't explain any of the long gaps.

## What the headings say about upgrading

Google labels each release's notes with section headings. "API Changes" appears
in 21 of the 32 releases. 20 of those are alphas; the other is 1.1.0-rc01.
"Bug Fixes" appears in 22 and "New Features" in 11, one of which is
1.1.0-beta02. "Security Fixes" appears once, in 1.1.0-alpha10.

So most of this library's history is alpha builds that change the API. In
practice:

1. **Pin an exact version.** 20 of the 26 alphas carry API changes, so a
   dynamic version range will move under you.
2. **Default to the stable line.** 1.1.0 is the only stable build. Move to a
   1.2 alpha when you need something only 1.2 has, and read every alpha's API
   Changes section when you bump.
3. **Check feature availability at runtime.** The background-read permission
   arrived "guarded by feature availability", and PHR shipped as experimental
   APIs. A library version that compiles is not the same as a device that
   supports the feature.
4. **Expect quiet periods.** Four gaps in this history run past 100 days, and
   each one ended with another release. Read a long silence as a prompt to
   check, not as abandonment. The same goes for the [React Native
   bridges](/blog/react-native-health-stale) built on top of the platform.
