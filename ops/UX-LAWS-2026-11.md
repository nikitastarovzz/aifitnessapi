# Twenty UX laws, applied to aifitnessapi.com (2026-11-11 to 2026-11-12)

The owner shared a list of twenty "UX laws to tell Claude for better design"
and asked for each to be researched, mapped onto this site, planned, built,
tested and merged, a few at a time. This file is the record: the source read
for each law, what it meant here, what shipped, and what was measured.

Binding like the other plans in ops/: gates green before every commit (`tsc`,
`build`, `qa`), every change through a pull request with auto-merge.

## Sources

Every law was read at its lawsofux.com page in the session, plus the primary
statement where one exists online: Nielsen's own "Jakob's Law of the Internet
User Experience" (jakobnielsenphd.substack.com), W3C "Understanding SC 2.5.8
Target Size (Minimum)" for Fitts's law, and the Hick 1952 / Hyman 1953 and
Fitts 1954 citations on Wikipedia. These hosts had to be added to the cloud
environment's allowlist; the WebFetch tool still fails on them there, so fetch
with curl through the proxy.

Traffic decisions use Search Console, sc-domain:aifitnessapi.com, pages,
2026-08-13 to 2026-11-10: 933 pages with impressions, 607 clicks.

The list as given repeats Postel's law (16 and 17). It was applied twice, to
the two kinds of input this site accepts: typed searches and typed addresses.

## What each law changed

1. **Hick's law.** Footer: 89 links as one cloud, 1,669px on a phone, became
   five titled groups that fold on phones (450px). Phone menu: 21 flat rows
   became 9.
2. **Fitts's law.** A 375px audit of 18 live templates listed every target
   under 44px outside running text; footer rows, logo, search, burger,
   Subscribe, breadcrumbs and the HealthKit identifier group links raised.
3. **Jakob's law.** The conventions were already there; the flat footer was
   the exception, now grouped columns.
4. **Law of proximity.** Page actions split into "use this page" and a
   labelled Share row; previous and next joined to "Keep reading".
5. **Miller's law.** Tools hub: 44 tools in two lists (one of 28) became seven
   named groups of 4 to 8 (`toolGroups`, which throws on a missing tool). The
   phone menu's guides became two chunks of 7 and 8.
6. **Doherty threshold.** See "Measured" below. `/search-index-pages.json`
   answers first; downloads start on intent; the palette code loads at idle.
7. **Von Restorff effect.** The answer capsule is the only green-tinted box on
   a spoke; promo boxes moved to the neutral surface.
8. **Minimize target distance.** A thumb-zone "Contents" button opens the
   section list as a bottom sheet below `xl`.
9. **Serial position effect.** Menus ordered by search clicks: Pricing (91),
   Alternatives (71), Motion (69), Integrate (49), Fitness APIs and Fix (40
   each). Desktop: Fitness APIs, Pricing, API Picker, Changes. Phone: the top
   sections first, Changes & deadlines last. Cookbook, Changes, Picker and
   the market map had no search clicks in the window.
10. **Peak-end rule.** Spokes end on the reading path and Next steps instead
    of a 700px citation form; "Not really" in the feedback widget offers a
    search.
11. **Zeigarnik effect.** `PathResume` on the 20 of 25 reading-path steps that
    use the article template: step N of M, the next page, progress and "Mark
    this step done" from the same browser-local store as `PathProgress`.
12. **Law of Prägnanz.** `ClusterHero` is the label alone below `sm` (89
    templates); the H1 sits about 70px higher on a phone.
13. **Law of similarity.** About 70 links in sentences outside the prose were
    told apart by colour alone (1.28:1, the footer funding link 1:1). A global
    rule in globals.css underlines them, skipping button and block links.
14. **Uniform connectedness.** Key facts hang from the answer capsule as one
    card; reading-path markers are joined by a line.
15. **Tesler's law.** The heading `#` shows on touch screens and a tap copies
    the section URL (`HeadingLinkCopy`, capture phase because the anchors
    render through next/link).
16. **Postel's law, addresses.** `src/proxy.ts` 308-redirects a path with a
    capital letter or a `.html` ending to the lowercase, extension-free page;
    its matcher keeps every other request off it. The 404 offers "Did you
    mean" from the page index when a page is close (`NotFoundSuggest`).
17. **Postel's law, searches.** `queryTokens` drops punctuation and
    possessives ("health-connect", "HealthKit?", "Garmin's"); `typoScore`
    accepts one edit up to seven letters and two from eight, swaps included,
    only when nothing matches exactly. Palette and /search share both.
18. **Parkinson's law.** The signup form puts its two required fields first
    and says so; the rest is labelled optional. Email inputs carry
    `autocomplete="email"` and `inputMode="email"` (the alerts form had
    neither).
19. **Occam's razor.** Next steps dropped its third card, a second newsletter
    ask on a page that already makes one.
20. **Pareto principle.** 74 pages (9.5% of the 778 with impressions, `www.`
    URLs folded into their apex page) earned 80% of clicks, nearly all on the
    article template. Every law above landed on that template; the 74 were
    audited on the final build (see below).

## Measured

Search, throttled phone (1.6 Mbps, 150 ms RTT, 4x CPU), median of three:
production before, the batch 2 build behind a gzip proxy after (production
serves Brotli, so the after figures are the pessimistic side).

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Tap to open search | 524 ms | 396 ms |
| Last keystroke to first result | 1,352 ms | 16 ms |
| Tap to first result | 2,467 ms | 875 ms |

## Pareto audit of the final build

The 74 pages, each loaded at 375px on a phone profile and scrolled, before the
last fixes: none scrolled sideways, none logged a console error, none shifted
layout above 0.05, every phone hero was the slim label, and every article with
three or more sections had the Contents button. It found three things, fixed
in the same pull request:

- The download callout on two hubs (`ClusterDownload`, seen on /devices) still
  used the answer capsule's green tint. Neutral surface now.
- The "Embed this table" toggle under every embeddable table was 22px tall.
  44px on phones, 32px from `sm`.
- The registry, repository and website links in /health-mcp-servers' table
  were 16px tall and stacked. 32px rows.

A rerun surfaced the next tier, also fixed here: the "Covered here" product
pills on spokes (22px, now 32px) and the folded vendor-page lists in
`VendorHubMenu` on the library and vendor pages (17px rows, now 32px).

Checkboxes inside the signup form's interest rows were also flagged at 16px;
each sits inside a label row about 40px tall, which is the real target, so they
were left. Inline links inside sentences are exempt from the target-size rule.

## Left alone on purpose

- The homepage keeps one filled CTA and one gradient report card; it already
  isolated its primary action.
- In-prose heading anchors and one list-item link remain outside the
  underline rule.
- The five reading-path steps on tool and reference pages do not carry the
  path card; those templates are not the article template.
- Clusters dropped from the phone menu (market map, accessibility, audio
  coaching, own-your-data) stay in the footer. The menu panel renders only
  while open, so it carries no crawl weight.
