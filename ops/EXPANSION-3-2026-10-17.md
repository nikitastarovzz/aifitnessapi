## Why these three

From Search Console for 2026-09-12 to 2026-10-09 (`data/gsc/latest.json`),
Bing for the same window, and eight searches in the session's search tool:

- **Commercial, vendor-named pages earn the clicks.** Clicks per page:
  /alternatives 2.9, /pricing 1.6, /fitness-apis 1.0, /motion 0.8,
  /integrate 0.75. Reference sections earn close to nothing:
  /health-connect-api 125 impressions and 0 clicks; /build, /learn,
  /architecture and /data at 0.13 or below.
- **Vendor names are the biggest unmet demand.** Garmin alone is 48 queries
  and 336 impressions. The narrow Garmin queries rank 4 to 10, but "garmin
  api" sits at 43, "strava api" at 54, "whoop api" at 40 and "fitbit api" at
  70, and Google splits "garmin api" and "fitbit api" across two or three of
  our pages, the split SERP-30 #1 fixed for "fitness api".
- **Motion is the best non-commercial section** (1,373 impressions, average
  position 7.3). People search MediaPipe model-file URLs and "movenet vs
  mediapipe"; the competition is dev.to posts, student blogs and READMEs.
- **Long, assistant-style buyer questions already reach us near the top.**
  "Which wearable health data api is best for corporate wellness programs?"
  at 2; "a single api to pull all my participants' fitbit data" at 2.8;
  "build my own fitbit integration or use a research platform?" at 2.

## 1. Vendor developer hubs

1.1 **Google Health API hub [A].** The Fitbit Web API turns off on
    2026-10-30 (Google's About page, read 2026-10-17). A generator reads the
    v4 discovery document and Google's docs and writes one page per data type
    (44 in Google's table on 2026-10-17: operations, scopes, record type,
    storage resolution, webhook and true-zero support, compatible devices,
    fields), plus the hub, scopes, rate limits, webhooks, errors and the
    Fitbit Web API mapping. A weekly workflow re-reads the discovery document
    and opens a PR when its revision changes.
1.2 **One menu per vendor [B].** Every page about a vendor (integrate,
    pricing, alternatives, compare, fix, libraries, migrate, the /apis entry)
    links the others, computed from the cluster data, and the
    /integrate/<vendor>-api guide becomes the hub because it is the page
    Google already shows for "<vendor> api".
1.3 **Hub sections for the existing vendors [B, then CI]** — Garmin, Strava,
    WHOOP, Oura, Fitbit, Polar, Terra, ExerciseDB: access and approval, auth
    and scopes, data types, webhooks, rate limits, what changed. On-site facts
    first; new facts from snapshots.
1.4 **New vendor hubs [CI]** — Withings, Samsung Health, Huawei Health Kit,
    Suunto, Coros, Wahoo, Dexcom, Concept2.
1.5 **What changed, per vendor [A for Google, CI for the rest].** Spec
    revisions and release notes feed /changes.
1.6 **Exercise content APIs inside the hubs [A for GitHub-hosted, CI].**
    ExerciseDB alternatives and pricing are 25 of the 166 clicks; wger and
    free-exercise-db publish on GitHub.
1.7 **Smart rings roundup [CI].**
1.8 **One metric across platforms [A, B]** — fold into the hubs: each Google
    Health data type links the matching /data metric page, HealthKit and
    Health Connect type.
    _Status 2026-10-17: the HealthKit and Wear OS ends are built, one page per type and generated only from data already on the site — 127 HealthKit identifier pages at /healthkit-identifiers/<slug> (113 of 121 quantity, 14 of 30 category, 0 of 6 characteristic types) and 25 of 65 Wear OS Health Services data type pages at /wear-os-data-types/<slug>. A HealthKit page links its Health Connect record and /data metric guide wherever the verified matrix names the type (13 types); no site data maps a Wear OS constant to either platform, so those pages say so instead of matching by name. The thin-page rule (at least two evidence-backed facts beyond the abstract and availability) holds back 30 and 40 types, which stay table rows; qa's TYPE-PAGE-RULE, TYPE-PAGE-EVIDENCE and TYPE-PAGE-UNLINKED enforce it. The Google Health data-type side waits on 1.1._
